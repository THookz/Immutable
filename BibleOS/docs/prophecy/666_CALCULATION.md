## Historical Evidence for "Vicarius Filii Dei"

### The Title's Usage

The title "Vicarius Filii Dei" (Vicar of the Son of God) has been used in papal documents and inscriptions. While the Catholic Church has downplayed this title in recent times (especially after the 666 calculation became widely known), historical evidence confirms its use:

1. **Donation of Constantine** (8th century forgery, but believed authentic for centuries) - Used similar language
2. **Decretum Gratiani** (12th century) - Canon law collection
3. **Lucius Ferraris, Prompta Bibliotheca** (1755) - Catholic encyclopedia states: "The Pope is of so great authority and power that he can modify, explain, or interpret even divine laws... The Pope can modify divine law, since his power is not of man but of God, and he acts as vicegerent of God upon earth."

### The Papal Tiara

Some claim the title "VICARIUS FILII DEI" was inscribed on the papal tiara (triple crown worn by popes). While photographic evidence is disputed, numerous witnesses from the 19th and early 20th centuries reported seeing this inscription.

**Whether or not it was on the tiara, the title itself is documented in Catholic sources.**
